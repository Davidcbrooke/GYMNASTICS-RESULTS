Finals 2026: looking back
Sunday 22 November · Redbourn Leisure Centre
 
Looking back: what went well, and what we're changing

I don't think many competitions share this kind of feedback, but I want the NGL to be open about how things went.

What went well

Timings

Right up until Level C beam, the timings proved that competitions don't have to go on all day. Even though we finished ahead of schedule, we could have finished 45 minutes earlier. This was the first time we'd run two competitions at the same time through Elevien, and I hadn't added the remote judge to the second one. After a call to Croatia midway through, it was sorted, but it caused a backlog. It won't happen again!

I'm so proud of the coaches in every club for delivering against such strict timings. You absolutely smashed it. My error aside, we would have completed a competition with 71 gymnasts in 3.5 hours. That should be the standard: more gymnasts competing, without 12-hour days for parents, coaches and judges.

Atmosphere

It felt incredible! As at all our competitions, parents and coaches kept telling us it felt ‘chill’ compared with other competitions, which is exactly what I wanted.

Parent and club support

This matters so much if people are going to keep organising events like this. I've travelled around the country to watch my own children swim a 27-second race at the British Finals, only to see them disqualified for a technical infraction with no reason given. It's hard to know how to react. On Sunday we had a few challenges, all made respectfully, and our judges gave constructive feedback explaining each decision. Many competitions don't allow this, but I think it worked well.

Overcoming adversity

A couple of gymnasts became overwhelmed on the day. We juggled things around and let them perform again. That's unusual at a competition, but I think it's the right thing to do. It shows young people that this is a safe environment to face their fears. You should be proud to be part of clubs that embrace new ideas like this.

Technology

Several new tools went live on Sunday, including a connected competition music player. Clubs uploaded music easily, and it fed straight into one player that ran the whole competition, including announcing gymnasts onto the floor. (Yes, that voice was AI!) AI is driving everything in the NGL at an incredible rate: the programmes, the coaching sheets, the medals. It will keep the NGL well ahead of other competitions. AI even wrote and designed this page and the email that brought you here.

Judging

Your gymnasts were remote-judged by a National judge in Dublin, which raises the standard of judging significantly. She also judged their beam routines at every competition this season. That consistency is a first in gymnastics, and it means gymnasts can track their progress more clearly than ever before. Gymnastics rules are still far too complex, though: judges have to find the right value from hundreds of possible skills in a matter of seconds. It's an incredibly difficult job and it needs to be simpler. We'll be making it easier in 2027.

What didn't go so well

How gymnasts qualified

A few parents have asked how, and who, qualified. When you design a competition, there's a trade-off between how long it runs and how many sub-divisions it has. The NGL wants competition within age groups (which is why the regional competitions have so many sub-divisions), but we've also committed to competitions usually finishing within 3 hours. One Final with 12 possible sub-divisions would have meant running finals over four days, which just wasn't possible in this pilot season.

I did want as many gymnasts from every age group as possible to take part, and they did. Having watched my own children be the youngest in a swimming team, then progress through the years until they led those teams, I can see the benefits. Parents of our younger gymnasts should be incredibly proud: their gymnasts had already beaten hundreds of older gymnasts to get their place. I also understand it may have felt unfair on the day. It's a trade-off, and I'm not sure I got it right, but I hope so.

For 2027
• Finalists will be selected on their all-around scores: the highest in each level. Much simpler.
• We'll look to add more sub-divisions within each level.
• Many more clubs are likely to compete next year, so places will be harder to win. Another reason to treat this pilot Finals as a unique experience for so many of the children.

Thank you for being part of the first NGL season. See you in 2027!
